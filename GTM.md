# Go-To-Market Notes

## Target Users

Initial focus:
- startup founders
- engineering managers
- small technical teams
- AI-native startups with 5–50 employees

These teams often adopt multiple AI tools quickly without centralized spend visibility.

## Core Problem

AI tooling adoption is becoming fragmented:
- multiple coding assistants
- overlapping subscriptions
- unclear seat ownership
- duplicated workflows

Most small teams do not have dedicated procurement workflows.

## Initial Distribution Strategy

### 1. Founder Communities

Potential channels:
- Hacker News
- Indie Hackers
- startup Slack communities
- engineering leadership groups

The product is lightweight enough to demo quickly through screenshots and shareable reports.

### 2. LinkedIn Content

Example topics:
- “How much are startups actually spending on AI tools?”
- “Why engineering teams accumulate overlapping copilots”
- “Common AI tooling waste patterns”

### 3. Shareable Audit Reports

Public audit URLs create lightweight viral distribution because teams can forward optimization reports internally.

## Product Positioning

The product is intentionally positioned as:
- lightweight
- fast
- operationally useful

Not:
- enterprise procurement software
- accounting infrastructure
- compliance tooling

## Why This Wedge Makes Sense

AI tooling adoption is accelerating faster than budgeting discipline.

Teams often optimize cloud spend aggressively while AI subscriptions remain decentralized and difficult to track.

## Early Success Indicators

Positive signals would include:
- repeat audits from the same teams
- report sharing between coworkers
- inbound requests for integrations
- usage concentrated among engineering managers

## Future Expansion Areas

Potential future directions:
- Slack integrations
- billing integrations
- historical spend tracking
- vendor benchmarking
- procurement workflows
